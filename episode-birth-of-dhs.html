The Birth of the Department of Homeland Security

After 9/11, the U.S. government undertook the largest federal reorganization since the creation of the Pentagon. The result was DHS — a sprawling mega-agency that absorbed immigration functions from three different departments. How did this reshuffling shape the immigration system we have today, and what did we lose in the process?

Claire Trickler-McNulty: Welcome back to the Melting Pod. Today we’re talking about something that fundamentally reshaped how America handles immigration — the creation of the Department of Homeland Security.

Doug Rand: Yeah, and I think most people don’t realize that before 2003, there was no DHS. Immigration was handled by the Immigration and Naturalization Service — the INS — which sat inside the Department of Justice.

Amanda Baran: And the INS was this fascinating, deeply dysfunctional agency that was responsible for both welcoming immigrants and deporting them. It was like having the same person be your defense attorney and the prosecutor.

Claire: Which is exactly why Congress decided to blow it up after 9/11. But the story of how DHS came together is really a story about the politics of fear, bureaucratic turf wars, and some genuinely consequential decisions made under enormous time pressure.

Doug: Let’s bring in our guest. Michael Chen served in senior roles at both the old INS and the new DHS during the transition. Michael, take us back to that moment…
